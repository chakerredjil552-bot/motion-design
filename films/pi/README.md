# π — a 90-second motion film

A wordless, neon motion-graphics film about the number π: where it comes from, how Archimedes cornered it, and where it lives today. Every frame and every sound is generated by code, with no stock footage, samples, or AI video. The only characters on screen are π, numerals and mathematical symbols.

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 90 s, 16:9, 60 fps |
| Output | 1920×1080 by default; `--scale 2` gives 3840×2160 |
| Palette | `#05060A` black, cyan `#5EF2FF`, violet `#8A5CFF`, gold `#FFC66B` |
| Look | additive neon lines, 3-level bloom, chromatic aberration, vignette, film grain, depth-of-field dust |
| Sound | synthesized pads, π-digit melody, sub-bass and cue-synced SFX |

## Shot list

| Time | Shot | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8 s | The Spark | A point ignites, pulses and sweeps out a perfect circle while a polar grid and HUD rings fade in. |
| 8–20 s | The Ratio | A golden diameter slices through, the circle rolls and unrolls its circumference, and three diameters lay end to end. The leftover gap resolves into `3.14159…`, then particles assemble **π**. |
| 20–32 s | Archimedes | Inscribed and circumscribed hexagons double 6 → 12 → 24 → 48 → 96. Live bounds squeeze in to `3.1408 < π < 3.1429`. |
| 32–45 s | Infinity of Digits | π explodes into a three-armed spiral galaxy made of real π digits (4,650 computed with Machin's formula), and **∞** traces itself at the vanishing point. |
| 45–58 s | Waves | The spiral collapses into a unit circle tracing a sine wave (with π, 2π… markers), then water ripples, sound waves and a heartbeat. |
| 58–72 s | Technology | Wireframe montage: a planet with an orbiting satellite, GPS triangulation, meshing gears, an arch bridge, a radio dish and a spoked wheel, each with a faint π watermark. |
| 72–82 s | The Equation | Everything implodes, then `e^{iπ} + 1 = 0` flies in symbol by symbol and locks with a flash and shockwave. |
| 82–90 s | Outro | The equation dissolves into one circle. The camera pulls back to reveal hundreds of circles forming a giant π, then fades to black. |

## Files

- `index.html`: an interactive player (space bar plays and pauses, the bar scrubs). With `?render=1` it is a headless frame source instead.
- `core.js`: the engine (3D camera and projection, neon strokes, sprites, bloom, chromatic aberration, grain).
- `shots.js`: the eight shots. Each frame is a pure function of `t`, so frames can be rendered in any order.
- `audio.py`: the score and sound design (numpy + scipy). Its cue times mirror `shots.js`.
- `render.js`: parallel Playwright/Chromium workers pipe JPEG frames into ffmpeg (x264), then mux the audio with loudness normalization.
- `fonts/`: Inter, JetBrains Mono and STIX Two Text, all under the SIL Open Font License.

## Build

```bash
pip install numpy scipy
python3 audio.py ../../outputs/pi_audio.wav
node render.js --workers 4 --out ../../outputs/pi_film_1080p60.mp4 --audio ../../outputs/pi_audio.wav
# 4K
node render.js --scale 2 --workers 4 --out ../../outputs/pi_film_4k60.mp4 --audio ../../outputs/pi_audio.wav
# a single section, e.g. the equation
node render.js --from 72 --to 82 --out ../../outputs/equation.mp4
```

`render.js` needs Playwright with Chromium and ffmpeg on the PATH. `--sub N` accumulates N sub-frames per frame as a shutter blur. It is off by default, because fast particles already draw their own light trails and sub-frames double them.

To preview, serve the folder (for example with `python3 -m http.server`) and open `index.html`. Put `pi_audio.m4a` next to it to hear sound.
